What is BEAR?

A concise practitioner model for communicating attacker progression

The Problem

Established frameworks provide depth, but responders and decision-makers also need a fast shared view of attacker progression. BEAR supplies that communication layer without replacing the underlying standards.

The Solution

BEAR cuts through the noise with four questions that matter:

B Did the adversary get in?
→
E How far did the adversary move?
→
A Did the adversary gain privileged control?
→
R What impact can the adversary create?
When to Use
🔴 Active Incident — Quickly assess where the attacker is and what's at risk
🟡 Red Team Debrief — Structure findings by impact, not just technique
🟢 Control Validation — Test defenses at each phase, find the gaps
🔵 Executive Briefing — Translate technical status into business risk
How to Use This Tool

Select a view mode below based on your role. Click any phase card to expand details. Toggle framework overlays to see mappings to MITRE, NIST, ISO, or CIS.

Mapping scope: These are representative practitioner alignments, not official crosswalks or one-to-one equivalences. NIST entries are outcomes rather than products; ISO Annex A selection remains risk-based; ATT&CK techniques are non-exhaustive. Priority labels are a starting point and must be tailored to organizational risk. Reviewed 2026-09-02 against ATT&CK v19.2, NIST CSF 2.0, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, and CIS Controls v8.1.
